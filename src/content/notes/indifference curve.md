# indifference curve
##

![[Pasted image 20240924143340.png]]
- At what different quantities would a consumer get the same [[utility]]. 
- This is like [[Production Possibility Frontier|PPF]] but ulta.

- [[characteristics of indifference curve]]

### Tradeoffs
![[Pasted image 20240924144154.png]]
- The tradeoff around 8 - 9 is very low because we would still want the 1 donut instead of just getting 0 donuts to get the same utility. 

### Steepness
- If the [[indifference curve]] is steeper than you would have to give up more goods  $y$  to get the same amount of $x$.
- If your [[indifference curve]] is not steep at all then you are willing to give up a lot of good y for good x. (you love good x).
	- So the [[relative value]] of good x is larger.

### Curvature
- ![[Pasted image 20240924153725.png]]
- In the second one when you have too many y then you are willing to give up many of them. This is in the case of [[complement econ|complement]]s because there you need both goods.
- The first one is the case of [[substitute]]s since you can replace with the other without much consequence.
- For perfect substitutes this would be a straight line
