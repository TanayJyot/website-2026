## Why top students don’t go to tuitions

Even as a kid I seemed to have noticed that students that go to after school tuitions are rarely the same as the students that are on top of their classes. I did not seem to find the answer until I reached University.

In my first year studying at UofT, the only course that affected my GPA was the notorious MAT137: Calculus with Proofs. The course is one of the most hated and talked about courses at UofT. Primarily because of the structure of the course, the steep curve as they try to reteach the basics. This course alone brought down my GPA from a 4.0/4.0 to a 3.43.

As much as everyone and I love to blame the course for its outcomes. We all also noticed another trend: the people that seemed to complete the problem sets of the course without any external help seemed to be doing rather well in the course. This was a rather staple thing to happen as one of the TAs later mentioned the same phenomenon in a Piazza post talking about how students seemed to take help too early on when they receive the problem sets because of which they did not **struggle** and those who did **struggle** seemed to be doing well on the course.

### These students have better genetics

I remember being told about in one of my psychology lectures about how the memory retention of students that made mistakes while doing topics remembered them better.  This does not make a lot of traditional sense to us because the people we see around us that do well seem to answer the questions immediately. They **struggled** so much with the concept before that now they get it better than everyone else.

We then assume that these people have better genetics or are in some way or form naturally better than everyone else around them and of course some of them are, but there is also a role of nature vs nurture.

I remember reading another study in the book How Luck Happens which shows how people that are obese by genetics seem to still be able to lose weight and retain it and people that are fit seem to gain weight easily, without much of a role of genetics to play and rather more of putting the work in and the effort itself.

This study (although having to do nothing with intellect) convinced me that it is upon us as people to change the way we act or the way we are genetically defined.

I come from a family of average students and many of my friends at UofT do so too. Because your parents weren’t the best academically and someone else’s were does not mean you cannot work your way to become as good as or in fact better than everyone else. We all struggled believe it or not and for all of us things got easier as we reached high school. Also, someone in each and every family struggles and starts this genetic chain of smart people before being again destroyed by someone who chooses to not use this intellect by **struggling**.

A lot of the people around us that seem to be doing well does not mean they have better genes, it just means that the **struggle** that they went through earlier makes things easier for them now. They build their foundation headstrong and struggled a lot while building this foundation in younger ages.

MAT137 being a course that rebuilds foundations requires the same kinds of struggles that were required when we first learnt about maths and numbers and operators and grammar. Many things that these top students **struggled** with at a younger age and the rest of us only see a reflection of this now.

### Why “struggling” matters

So how does being a top student and going to a tuition relate to struggling?

You learn a concept better when you struggle with it and going to a tuition eliminates the process of struggling. Let me show you how

#### The life of a typical tuition kid

A typical tuition going student life looks like going to school, then going to tuition, doing some sort of sports and doing something entertaining with the rest of your time.

The school is basically an introduction of each and every topic. It assumes you to be well versed with topics taught in the past and introduces new ones on the basis of that. Students that have struggled with the old topics understand them well and keep performing well and those that don’t keep hanging back.

After this first introduction, a typical tuition student goes to the tuition, informs them about what they were taught in school and the tuition teacher gives them another introduction to the topic or helps them solve questions based on the topic.

Here are where the first problems arise, if a tuition reintroduces the topic, then there is only passive learning happening yet which defeats the whole point of spending the limited time you have after going home to **struggle** into understanding the topic yourself which is what top students do. The passive learning part and knowing the basics were already meant to be covered in school and students, knowing that they would be doing the topic again put less effort first time. Doing the same thing twice in the same manner means that if you were meant to get a 100% at school then you would end up getting 30% of what you could get at school and 70% in the tuition for double the amount of time spent. This in itself isn’t bad but the problem is what happens later in terms of time spent self studying which I will talk about later.
