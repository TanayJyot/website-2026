## Introduction

Hi, I am Tanayjyot Singh Chawla, I recently took part in a digital detox experiment as a part of ‘The McLuhan Seminar in Creativity and Technology’. Recently, I have embraced a studious and diligent lifestyle as I am enrolled in a challenging and rewarding Computer Science program. I also attempt to take time out for extracurricular activities and social aspects that hone my ability to learn. These aspects also contributed to my daily routine as I planned to work on preclass material and schedule events in my calendar. I was not always successful as I would not realise where I was wasting my time and would get distracted more easily.

However, back home my lifestyle used to be different as it consisted of meditating and exercising regularly which seems to be a luxury at the University of Toronto. I also had a lifestyle which was based on application rather than theoretical knowledge as I worked in the office with my father. The exposure allowed me to come up with ideas frequently complemented by flexible hours and deadlines.

During the digital detox, I had a trip to Waterloo planned for which I had gotten the ticket in my friend’s phone as well as a Diwali event for which I had printed the tickets before the detox.

___

## Report

### Day 1

- The moment I gave up my phone I had a Mathematics Recognised Study Group event planned for which I headed directly to the library where we worked on questions and I started to notice how everyone around me became distracted immediately as soon as we reached a mental block.
- I walked back with friends for the Diwali event, wherein I got the impulse to check my phone every few minutes. I also felt the impulse to take photos which I was able to avoid.
- I journaled and tried reading a book but I felt tired and slept immediately. I had a prolonged sleep that night.

### Day 2

- I started my day feeling fresh and energetic. I had the impulse to check my phone immediately but I  chose to read a book instead. I ended up spending a lot of time on the book.
- After completing my daily chores. I meditated until I could resolve certain ideas as I had taken away the means to pull out by not timing my session. Then, I cleaned my room and put things in order. I also changed the arrangements of my room making them more methodical. Something that would bother me every time I entered my room. I also played guitar and exercised. I took a nap in the evening and as I did not keep track of time or see the time I was sleeping I slept for longer hours than usual and had a more refreshing sleep.

### Day 3
- I started the day by meeting the rest of my friends at a nearby coffee shop. We took the train to Waterloo and since I did not have my phone we played cards on the way there. I had a lower urge to use my phone and started to enjoy my time even more without it. We then took a bus to the UWaterloo campus where we took a campus tour. At some point, I had lost all the impulse to look at my phone and almost forgotten about the fact that I didn’t have it with me.
- I unwrapped my phone at 5 pm in Waterloo and then headed back home.

## Reflections

- During the duration of the digital detox, my thoughts felt less constrained and more refined because my mind was more often able to get into the chaos state[^1] since I was less stimulated. I was also able to observe what I was doing more closely because of which it became easier for me to decide what I spent my time on. Thus, allowing me to spend my time more judiciously and complete many activities that I would presume would take me hours.

- The access to chaos also allowed me to come up with more technical ideas as I was able to form connections that I would otherwise take for granted. I realised that I lost many such connections because whenever things would get difficult to keep thinking about I would tend to distract myself with my phone.

- I felt more “conscious” of the decisions I made throughout the detox. It was as if I was the one governing whatever I did rather than letting an external force determine what I do. As I had less exposure to the constant stream of thoughts everyone else had on social media, each of my decisions felt personal and I felt completely responsible for whatever I did.

- Since I did not stimulate myself with my phone the moment I woke up it was easier for me to start working and it also became easier to be focused because even though I got impulses to be distracted, I was able to ignore these impulses and keep making progress towards the task I was working on until I burnt out.

- I was also able to take note of what was happening in my environment more closely and when I felt certain kinds of emotions (the feeling my room would give me) I figured out how to rectify them. When I walked with my friends, I also observed the beauty of the little things in the environment that surrounded me.(The squirrels and the changing colour of the trees) and the fact that I was walking slower to keep up with everyone almost made me feel like it was upon me to take a step back and wander.

- During the Diwali event, I felt the impulse to click photos at multiple moments, but I soon realised that I only got such an impulse when I watched someone else click one. I realised that even when we are using our phones, at some moments we pick it up just to fit in with everyone around us is doing so and not because of an innate desire to do so.

- When I was reading I was able to be more engaged with the story and engage with the content more thoroughly. It was as if I experienced a higher attention span because I could access the flow state[^6] more easily. This was an ability I used to have when I was not as engaged on my phone as much. I also felt less bored while cleaning my room or eating food. It is as if the mundane tasks became suddenly more interesting because the more interesting thing I engaged with was not with me.

- As the nights approached, I felt sleepy gradually until I fell asleep since I could let go of my habit of watching videos on my phone before I slept.

- Through this I observed that I did have time for completing tasks and I did not need to forego my habits as I noticed that I was spending a lot less time on activities than I thought while getting similar outputs, which is something that resonates with Parkinson’s Law[^3]. Since I chose to not keep track of time in most scenarios, it was fascinating for me to observe how clueless I was. I realized that I have had a lower level of awareness in these past few months because of which I had been making unfruitful choices.

- I also felt more engaged in conversations, as I was less distracted and I took learning from other people more seriously because at that point they were my only source of information.

- I also realised that the phone became more of a coping mechanism whenever I was in an unfavourable situation because of which I had started to rely more on it as I entered University. It let me ignore the real world and think everything was alright without actually working on things which would only make things worse in the long run since reality is the only thing that’s real[^4]

- I experienced how the things I would call distractions weren’t distractions, they were overstimulation.[^5] There was so much to do that not doing anything that caters to my tasks felt anti-productive and then I would think that going through my phone would be the external reward. Instead of allowing my tasks to reward me, I would go back to my phone to be rewarded. As I went through the vicious cycle I stopped noticing how my phone was equally anti-productive.

- In a future detox, it would be better to find apps that act as stimuli and eliminate them as there are a lot of quality-of-life things that the phone helps with, such as getting directions and web searches or convenience like Bikeshare considering the benefits to be achieved are similar to those that I did. In the current era, eliminating a mobile device is not practical as it is not a natural state for a person to work in. I had to make sure I was safe at all times during the detox since I did not have a means to contact others which could be restricting. Another alternative could be to use a feature phone so that most of the basic tasks such as communication are still possible.

- If you are considering a detox I would advise you to start limiting your phone usage from a week before the detox so that you get to enjoy the detox more as you would naturally have fewer impulses to use your phone by then.

___
## Conclusion

I did not expect a digital detox to have such a profound impact on various aspects of my life but considering the aspects we allow our phones to govern in our lives, it makes logical sense. The detox made me realise that the phone adds a level of pretence to our personalities. We become the way we use our phones. If we learnt to use our phones in a balanced manner we would be able to bring out uses that outweigh the costs of using our phones.

I also noticed that I accentuated particularly on my phone in my reflections and reports since I tend to shut off all of my other devices except my phone. It is also the only device that is the easiest to access which makes it easier to be addicted to.

I would conclude by saying that our phones are astonishingly powerful mediums, and our subjective usage determines the message.[^7]
