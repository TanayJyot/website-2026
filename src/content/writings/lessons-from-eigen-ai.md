## Who came, and why

EigenAI as a conference does not seem to obey the laws of a typical conference. People don’t care about panels as much nor do they care about networking, or at least most of them don’t. They somehow care about learning which is what they attribute to workshops.

The EigenAI signups were probably more so treating the conference like a hackathon in terms of signups out of enthusiasm to go to your first conference rather than a rational decision made out of “who is coming”, which is probably why some of the higher tier speakers did not get much retention anyways because people didn’t know they were “high tier”. But the other side of it is also that people didn’t know about these speakers early enough for them to do something about it. So the  audience that would come taking into account who was at the panel was not even present because they weren’t there.

## Sessions and panels

I feel mixed about having sessions in parallel. On one side there is the club prospect that not having enough people causes reputational harm / damage but on the other hand maybe there is some gain to students choosing what they want to do.

What is highly peculiar though is how students were highly interested to learn which is understandable because if I was in your first year then I would have also cared more about learning stuff first rather than networking with people / panelists.

The panels themselves were somewhat repetitive (in the 5 mins that I was there in them for) because they would come to some levels of generic advice that you could get either way. Person 1 and Person 2 pointed out how it was important for the moderator to be personally invested in the topic for them to be able to ask good questions. I think that scripts work in a professional setting but they require someone that cares which isn’t unnatural.

## Knowing your audience

One of the biggest things is being more cautious about your target audience. At conferences like WISE and ConferenceX, people come with a rationale behind why they are there and they can self-guide themselves to what they really wanna do at some time. ConferenceX doesn’t require that kind of domain knowledge but its reputation precedes it. With EigenAI you wanna guide students towards what is best for them rather than have them choose because they do not necessarily know what is good for them because they have just entered University.

## Pricing and attrition

The sweet spot of money also gains a lot more importance here, most UofT conferences charge around $30 - $50 and they don’t have the same kind of attrition EigenAI did and there may be a reason why that price range is good because it encourages people to at least come.

Considering that there were around 270 people on day 1 and a lot less on Day 2. We sold 450 tickets which bring us to a 40% attrition rate which is more typical of a hackathon. I also wonder if there is a natural cap to the amount of interest you can get for a beginner level conference centered around UofT. I don’t believe this to be true though because there are students from multiple majors that would be interested in something like this, I think a lot of the EigenAI attrition was caused by the lack of information.

Attrition and attendance are also not explicitly in our control so we will also have to make sure that we plan for contingencies if they don’t go our way.

## Marketing

One very easy way is to bring in high school students, I know Person 3 or Person 4 pointed out to this last minute but there wasn’t much at that point to add on to, but this is something that can be highly emphasized for AI^2 but that would require marketing to make it explicit that you don’t need experience for this.

There were a bunch of social media posts but those don’t mean anything without more information and that information came very last minute, which doesn’t give the audience the time to decide if this is somewhere that they wanna be.

Hiring could have been a very big incentivizer for people to come on Day 2 if it was advertised or mentioned properly on Day 1. I know someone asked me about this and I guided them to Person 5, because I had told them the previous day that hiring would happen today. I think this was in general something first years wanted but couldn’t have and could have been emphasized a lot better. It brings back to how big of a pillar marketing is in running anything.

## Building from the ground up

I think UTMIST is in a very interesting spot where companies now do care about us and we can get big names to come in and it is popular but we also need to ensure that we achieve the scale that we promise during events. There is a lot more carefulness and preplanning that we need to do when it comes to things now.

It is also very important to build things from the ground up, operating a conference or hackathon would not matter unless you first have a strong pull to incentivise people to come to begin with. Creating intrinsic motivation for someone to come somewhere requires us to first give them something of value. In case of a conference, this is getting to talk to people that would be difficult to access otherwise. The value prop is making something more accessible. Because EigenAI marketing was delayed, the value of the conference itself wouldn’t be visible to a rational person.

You can have the best organization and operations but none of that would matter if you don’t give people value for them to step into the conference. Incentive effects are real and they matter a lot. Building from the ground up means to get those speakers or prizes or some sort of value add going first and is more important than the logistical details of the event. Operations is built on top of that grounding.

## Leadership

There are also some leadership lessons here, it is very important to have someone that can rally volunteers up to work and tell them why what they are doing right now is important. Volunteers are a very essential part of an event, but your intrinsic motivation isn’t reflected in them so you need to delegate and explain to them why things really matter to get them to be motivated. You need a face associated with the event that people respect and trust.

It is also important to get people invested in the event by involving and giving them more ownership over it. There wasn’t a lot of ownership transfer during EigenAI to Senior Execs although some of the Senior Execs can be really good at handling particular things and a lot of different ways people could be divided into teams as “Volunteer Leaders” and given more context to do stuff. And these things come out of instinct, intuition and observation to be able to act quickly and make decisions rather than something you can prepare for. If something isn’t working then changing it on the spot is very important because while an event is running, the most important thing is to make sure everything is in motion in the way it is supposed to be rather than a confined set of rules. It is also about delegating responsibilities to people so that you can concentrate on how level things as a leader.

Person 1 mentioned how brainstorming would be a good way to ensure that people are invested and they care about the event. There needs to be some level of ownership by people for someone to care and step up. That ownership needs to come from a lot of different people to have something  run. You also need to be able to gauge people as a leader to figure out who is going to be someone that can take ownership and lead people, who likes to do what activity based on what you know about them. You have to know how to get people to their best potential.

> A great leader knows how to identify and navigate around the team members' weaknesses and capitalize on their strengths. At PayPal, I remember always being blown away by the ability of my co-founder, Peter Thiel's, ability to extract stellar results from people who were often dismissed by others as "too hard to work with" or "too weird" by zeroing in on their unique abilities very quickly and putting them in situations where that very ability had maximum leverage attached to it.

The Pygmalion effect all over again

## The volunteers

I don’t think the volunteers not working is their fault, they were just lost without a sense of direction. They wouldn’t be there if they didn’t wanna help. I think a lot of these things aren’t about execution but to create a learning experience that everyone can also enjoy and that is difficult to replicate.
